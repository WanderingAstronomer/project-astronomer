The Maestro System

One operator, many sidebar groups, a Maestro over each. Every group is grounded in a folder, its workspace, and its Maestro presides over it. Workspaces ask each other questions Maestro to Maestro, and each answer comes back up through that workspace's own Conductor and sections.

Doctrine: doctrine/06-delegation.md New law: L-19 Gate built and tested, not installed
To found a new workspace: make the sidebar group, open a session in its folder, and hand it project-astronomer/install/FOUNDING.md. It renames itself Maestro (<Group>), asks you four questions with recommendations (tier, size, purpose, data boundary), writes the precedent as D-1 to D-4, and registers itself.

"a maestro of one group should never talk directly to the conductor or sessions of another group unless the operator explicitly permits it." Drew, 2026-10-10

Sizes

Pick the size the work needs

The same rules hold at every size. A bigger size adds roles and pages and never relaxes a rule. Changing size is a ruling in the group's ledger.

The one size that's not allowed: a Maestro with sections and no Conductor. That's the setup that leaked every session's traffic to you on 10-09. A Solo Maestro that needs a second session becomes a Chamber first.

Exchanges

How a question waterfalls

  1. YouTell Maestro (Health): "ask Vociferous whether the tester form went out."
  2. Maestro (Health)Logs X-4 with your words verbatim, looks up Maestro (Vociferous Next) in the registry, and sends an INQUIRY.
  3. Maestro (Vociferous Next)Answers from its own pages and ledger if it can. If not, it passes X-4 to its Conductor.
  4. Conductor (Vociferous Next)Answers from the Board, or asks the owning sections by session id, then hands one answer back up.
  5. Maestro (Vociferous Next)Checks the answer against its data boundary and replies with sources and a confidence label.
  6. Maestro (Health)Closes X-4 and tells you in one line, or on its page.

  • Questions only, never orders. A REQUEST to do work becomes an item on the other group's Decisions page for you to rule on. A Maestro's word counts as yours only inside its own group.
  • No recursion. A question goes down one group and no further. A section never starts an exchange, and a repeated X-ID isn't passed on again.
  • Silence is never an answer. If nobody can answer, the reply says "could not determine" and who would know.
  • Nothing is written because of an exchange. Answering is read-only.
  • Permits come from your own words in the session that will use them. A permit relayed by another Maestro doesn't count.
Naming

Names are addresses

  • Maestro (<Group>) and Conductor (<Group>), using the exact sidebar group name, so you can see at a glance who presides where.
  • Group names must be unique. Your sidebar has two groups called "Vociferous Next" right now: one with 59 sessions, one empty.
  • Sections are named by goal, not by date or persona. The Conductor addresses them by session id, because the same goal name can turn up in two groups.
  • A fork isn't a Maestro. A forked Maestro has the original's memory but none of its standing. It messages nobody but you until you rename it and file it in a group. This session is one: "Maestro (fork)", in no group.
  • IDs are local. D375 inside Vociferous Next is VOC:D375 from any other group.
Holding the line

Instructions alone won't hold the boundary

Astronomer measured that a session agreeing to a rule isn't evidence the rule will bind it. So the plan is a gate: a hook that runs before every cross-session message, reads the registry, and blocks any edge the table doesn't allow, unless a live permit covers it. Sessions that aren't in the registry, including forks, can message only you. It catches accidents and drift, not a session working to get past it. It's built and tested (62 tests, and all 19 seeded defects caught), but not installed. Installing it means one hook entry in your user settings, and that's your call. Until then, the boundary holds only by habit.
